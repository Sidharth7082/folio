---
title: The First Keeper
---

She was small and old and entirely unimpressed.

"Sit," said the first keeper. "You're dripping on my floor, and my floor is nine hundred years of other people's mistakes, so I'd like it to stay dry."

Sela sat. The lantern sat with her, on the table between them, and for the first time since the harbour it was completely, unremarkably quiet.

"You want to know why the flame asks questions," the first keeper said. "That's the wrong question. The flame doesn't ask. It listens. It has been listening since the beginning, to every keeper who ever carried it, and every keeper who ever carried it has told it the same thing."

"What did we tell it?"

"That we would rather be told what to do."

Sela thought about the oath. Four hundred words. Three lines inked over so carefully that only an angled lantern could find them.

"That's why you're still here," Sela said slowly. "You're not the first keeper. You're the last one who said no."

The old woman smiled for the first time, and it was not a kind smile, but it was an honest one, and Sela had not seen an honest smile in a very long time.

"Now," she said, "we can begin."
