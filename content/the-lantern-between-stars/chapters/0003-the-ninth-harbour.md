---
title: The Ninth Harbour
---

The ninth harbour was not on any chart because it had been built out of ships.

Sela counted forty-one hulls before she lost her nerve and stopped counting. They were lashed together with rope the colour of bone, deck to deck, mast to mast, until the whole thing stood above the water like a city that had been dropped and had decided not to break.

Lights moved along the walkways. Not lantern-light. Something colder and steadier, the light of things that have been thinking for a very long time.

There was a woman waiting at the edge of the first deck, holding a rope, as if she had been waiting for a specific boat and this was simply the one that had finally arrived.

"You're late," the woman said.

"I didn't know I was expected."

"Everyone is expected. That's the trouble with prophecies." The woman looked at the lantern, and something in her face closed, like a door in a house where the arguments have all been had already. "You listened to it."

"It asked me a question."

"Yes. It does that." The woman stepped back and gestured along the walkway, toward a tower of stacked decks at the centre of the city, where the cold light was brightest. "It always asks the ones who might say yes. Come inside, keeper. There's someone who wants to meet you, and she has been remarkably patient."
