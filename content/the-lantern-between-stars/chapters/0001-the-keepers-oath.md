---
title: The Keeper's Oath
---

The lantern room smelled of salt and old brass, the way it had smelled on every morning of Sela's apprenticeship. She had swept that floor ten thousand times. She knew where the third plank creaked, which window fogged first when the tide turned, and how the flame leaned inland just before a storm.

None of that prepared her for the silence.

"You will carry it to the far shore," the Harbourmaster had said, and then he had not said anything else, because there was nothing else to say. The oath was four hundred words long and every one of them was older than the city.

Sela read it twice. The first time she read it as a keeper, the way she had been taught. The second time she read it as someone who intended to survive, and she noticed what her teachers had never mentioned: three lines near the end that had been inked over, so carefully that only a lantern held at an angle could show them at all.

*The flame is not a burden. The flame is a question.*

She did not mention the lines to the Harbourmaster. He was old, and tired, and he had already given her the only answer he had.

She took the lantern down from its hook. It weighed less than it should have.

Outside, the whole harbour had come to watch. Ten thousand lanterns on ten thousand masts, and every one of them lit by a flame she had tended since she was nine years old. They were beautiful. She had never hated anything so much in her life.

The boat was small and plain, with one sail and no name. That was the custom. Names belonged to things that came back.

Sela stepped in. The rope was cast off. The wind took the sail the way a hand takes a letter it has been waiting for.

And behind her, very quietly, the lantern began to hum.
