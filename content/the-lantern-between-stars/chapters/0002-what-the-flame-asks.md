---
title: What the Flame Asks
---

The humming stopped when she answered it.

Sela had not meant to answer. She had meant to sit very still and count the waves, the way you were supposed to, and to keep her hands away from the glass. But the sound had a shape to it, and the shape had a question in it, and before she knew what she was doing she had said, out loud, to a lantern, in the middle of an empty sea:

"Who lit the first one?"

The flame went flat and blue. Then it rose, and it was not blue anymore, it was the colour of a road at dusk, and Sela understood that she had been given an answer, and that the answer was a place.

She had grown up believing the far shore was a metaphor. That was the polite version, the one they taught in the harbour school. The impolite version, which the dockworkers told each other when the tide was low, was that the far shore was where the last keeper had gone, and that the last keeper had gone there nine hundred years ago, and that nobody had ever come back to correct the record.

The lantern did not care which version she believed. It only wanted to be carried in the direction it was pointing, and the direction it was pointing was not the direction the map said.

Sela looked at the map for a long time. Then she folded it, and put it under the bench, and turned the tiller.

The stars above her rearranged themselves, very slightly, the way a crowd shifts when someone important walks into the room.

"Well," she said, to nobody. "At least it isn't boring."
