---
title: A Question Worth Carrying
---

They talked until the cold lights dimmed and the tide under the lashed hulls turned and turned like a slow argument.

By morning — or what passed for morning out here, where the sun was a rumour — Sela understood three things.

The first was that the flame was not a fire. It was a record. Every keeper who had ever said *tell me what to do* had left something in it, a small grey stone of a decision, and the stones had piled up over nine hundred years until the light they gave off was so bright that no one could look directly at what it was made of.

The second was that the far shore was real, and that reaching it would not end the tradition. It would end the *need* for it. That was worse, in the way that being cured is worse than being ill, because being cured means you have to decide what to do with the rest of your life.

The third was the only one that mattered to her.

"Take it back," the first keeper said, at the end. "Or don't. Both are answers. That's the whole point, and it took me nine centuries to learn it."

Sela picked up the lantern. It weighed exactly what it was supposed to weigh now, which is to say: as much as a question you have decided to carry yourself.

She stepped into the boat. The rope was cast off. The stars, above her, went back to being stars.

Behind her, the ninth harbour began, very slowly, to come apart into ships.
