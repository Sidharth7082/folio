---
title: Eleven Hours Early
---

The alarm that woke Ivo Ren was not the one for arrivals.

He knew all the station's alarms the way a farmer knows weather. There was the flat chime for a supply drone, the rising three-note for a solar flare, the ugly buzz for hull stress. This was none of those. This was the low, patient tone the station used when it wanted to be understood rather than heeded, and in two hundred and six days it had never once used it.

He found the message waiting in the queue, flagged by the protocol filter.

`ORIGIN: KESTREL-9 RELAY`
`DESTINATION: DEEP RELAY NETWORK, NODE 4`
`TIMESTAMP: 2087-114 09:41:06`

Ivo read it three times. Then he pulled up the station clock, because he wanted to be certain, and being certain is a thing you do slowly when you are alone.

The station clock said 2087-113 22:41:06.

Eleven hours.

The message had come from his own transmitter, and it would not be sent for eleven hours.

He sat down in the operator's chair, which was the only chair, and which he had come to think of as his in a way he did not like to examine. Protocol was very clear. An anomalous transmission is logged, sealed, and reported on the next scheduled uplink.

Ivo logged it. He sealed it.

Then he opened it again, and read what he had written, and read what the station had said he would write, and understood that they were the same, word for word, including the part where he hadn't written it yet.
