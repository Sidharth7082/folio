---
title: What Ivo Ren Sent
---

At 09:40 he was still arguing with himself, and losing, which is the only way to argue with yourself successfully.

At 09:41 he put his hands on the console.

The coordinates in the comment block resolved to a point eleven light-minutes out, in the dark between stars, where nothing had ever been and nothing was scheduled to be. The time resolved to now. The instruction resolved to a choice he had been circling for nine hours, the way you circle a word in a language you thought you spoke.

He could send the message. Protocol said log, seal, report. Protocol was written by people who had never been alone with a question.

Or he could send *his* message. Not the one the transmitter was going to produce. His own. Four words, addressed to the same empty point of space, into the same relay, at the same moment the station's clock rolled over to 09:41:06.

He chose.

The transmitter woke, and spoke, and went quiet, and the station's log recorded exactly one transmission at 09:41:06 — which is what the flagged message had always said it would record, and which is why, when the relief crew arrived four months later, the only thing out of place on Kestrel-9 was a cup of tea gone cold on the operator's desk, and a filter that had produced, by the station's own records, exactly zero flagged messages.

Forty years later the same comment block appeared in the same place in every relay in the network, and in every one of them it was still waiting, and in every one of them it was still, quietly, correct.

Ivo Ren did not come back. That is not the same as Ivo Ren being gone, and the difference is the entire point.
