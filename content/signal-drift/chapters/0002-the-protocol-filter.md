---
title: The Protocol Filter
---

The protocol filter was two hundred lines of very boring code and it had never once been interesting.

Ivo had read it on his first day, the way you read the terms of a lease you have already signed. It matched message headers against a table. If the header matched, the message was routed. If it didn't, the message was flagged. There was no third outcome. The filter had been in continuous operation for forty years and had produced, by the station's own records, exactly zero flagged messages.

Now it had produced one, and the one it had produced was the message Ivo was going to send.

He opened the filter's source, which took eleven seconds, because two hundred lines of very boring code is still two hundred lines when you are the only person awake within nine light-minutes.

He found the table. He found the routing rules.

Then he found, at the very bottom, in a comment block that had been dated the year he was born:

*Note: this filter is not for the network. This filter is for the operator. If it flags a message, log it, seal it, and read the next line.*

The next line was not a comment.

The next line was a set of coordinates, and a time, and a single instruction: *Do not send the message.*

Ivo looked at the timestamp on the flagged message. 09:41:06.

He looked at the station clock. 23:58:11.

Somewhere in the next nine hours and forty-three minutes, he was going to sit down at this desk and transmit something. The filter already knew what it was. The filter had known for forty years, and had been waiting, patiently, for someone finally to ask why it existed.

Ivo did not go to bed. He made tea instead, which on Kestrel-9 is a decision, not a habit, and he sat back down.
